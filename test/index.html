Getting more out of a medical visit

PACE is a set of four things patients can do during a visit, developed by communication researcher Donald Cegala. Read these — then you'll practice them in a short role-play tailored to you.

P
Present — describe your situation clearly and completely: when it started, how often, how bad, what you've tried, and what you hope the visit accomplishes.
"It started six weeks ago, it's most afternoons, and ibuprofen barely touches it."
A
Ask — ask the questions you actually have, about the diagnosis, tests, medications, or the plan.
"What's that test for, and how will the result change what we do?"
C
Check — verify you understood: restate the plan in your own words, ask what a term means, summarize before you leave.
"So just to check — twice a day with food, and come back in two weeks even if I feel fine?"
E
Express — voice concerns, doubts, and anything that would keep you from following the plan.
"Honestly, I'm worried it's something serious — and I'm not sure I can afford that option."
You'll practice in a made-up visit built around a topic you choose. A coach may quietly chime in with tips, and you'll get a summary at the end to keep. Please don't enter real personal health details you wouldn't want in a practice tool — the scenario only needs the general shape of your situation.
