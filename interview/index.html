Preparing for a medical visit

Office visits are short, often about 15 minutes. Preparing ahead helps you make the most of that time. Researchers have developed four guidelines for patients, called PACE, as an easy way to organize your feelings, questions, and concerns before a visit.

P
Provide information about how you feel.
Write down your symptoms and concerns before the visit. Note when things started, how often they happen, and what you have tried. Think about what you hope your health care professional can do for you.
"Is the pain constant, or does it come and go?"
"Does anything make it better or worse, like activity, food, or medication?"
A
Ask questions if you don't have enough information.
Think of your questions before the appointment and write them down. If you have more than three, star the most important ones so you ask those first.
"What is this condition, and why do I have it?"
"What side effects should I watch for, and what should I do if I miss a dose?"
C
Clarify what you hear.
Medical visits pack in a lot. Ask for simpler words when you need them, repeat instructions back in your own words, and review what you heard before you leave. If you might forget something, ask them to write it down.
"I don't understand. Can you say that again, using simpler words?"
"I think you're telling me that I should take this twice a day. Is that right?"
E
Express any concerns you may have.
Speak up about worries with your condition or the plan. If a treatment is hard to follow or has side effects, be honest instead of quietly stopping. There is often another option that fits you better.
"I'm not sure I can manage this every morning. Is there another way to take it?"
"That side effect worries me. What are my options?"
