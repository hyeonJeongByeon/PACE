## Before the session
- Send the participant link (`…/PACE/test2/?pass=<passcode>`) and ask them to
  share their screen.
- Confirm recording/consent per protocol.

## At the education screen
> "These four cards are a way of thinking about what patients can *do* in a
> visit — telling your story, asking, checking you understood, and saying
> what's worrying you. Take a minute to read them; we'll practice all of this
> in a role-play in a moment, so don't worry about memorizing."

## At the survey
> "This is like the check-in form before a real appointment. You can use a
> real health topic or a made-up one. Whatever you enter shapes the visit
> you're about to practice. Skip anything you'd rather not enter — and please
> don't put in personal details you wouldn't want in a practice tool."

(If they hesitate at a free-text box, point at the grey cue line under it.)

## At the briefing ("Your situation")
> "Read the situation box like it's the moment before the exam-room door
> opens. The blue box shows what's already in your chart — the doctor walks in
> knowing that, so you don't need to repeat it. Everything else is yours to
> bring up. Three ground rules: it's all typed chat; don't look things up
> outside — asking the doctor IS the exercise; and when the visit wraps up,
> you'll click one button to see your summary."

## During the role-play
- Do not prompt PACE behaviors; think-aloud probes only.
- The timer turns red in the last 2 minutes — that pressure is intentional.
- If they ask "should I end it?": "The doctor will wrap it up — you can keep
  going until then, or use Exit practice if you want to stop."

## At the summary
> "Take your time reading. Then walk me through: what feels accurate, what
> feels off, and what you'd want to see instead."
- Ask them to download the summary (and print if useful) before closing.
